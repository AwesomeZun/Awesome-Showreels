# A gut bacterial metabolite eases anxiety-like behaviour in mice

*Article · fictional manuscript written for this example · all data simulated (paper-src/simulate.py)*

Mira Okonkwo¹, Daniel Haas¹, Yuki Tanabe² & Leila Farahani¹ ✉ · ¹Department of Gut–Brain Physiology (fictional) · ²Behavioural Core

## Abstract
Mice whose gut bacteria were depleted with antibiotics lose a metabolite made by one group of gut bacteria, BXM-2 (a
fictional compound for this example), and spend less time in the open. Giving BXM-2 in the drinking water for 14 days
brought their time in the centre of an open field back from 8.9% to 19.2%, without changing how far they walked or how
fast they learned the water maze. Twelve mice per group (six female, six male), the smallest group powered for the
primary outcome.

## Animal welfare (the 3Rs)
- **Replacement.** The metabolite's effect on neurons was first screened in cultured cells; mice were used only for the
  behaviour no cell model shows.
- **Reduction.** Twelve per group from a power calculation on the pilot; the water maze reused the same animals.
- **Refinement.** Handling with a tunnel, habituation to the room, enrichment in every cage, no food restriction.
All procedures under an approved animal protocol (fictional, for this example).
