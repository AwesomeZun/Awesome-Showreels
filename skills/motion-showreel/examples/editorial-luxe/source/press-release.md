# MAISON VEYRANDE

## LES HEURES BLANCHES

HAUTE COUTURE · AUTOMNE–HIVER 2026–2027

*Communiqué de presse · Paris, 6 July 2026 · Embargoed until 20:00 CET*

---

Between midnight and dawn, the atelier keeps its own hours.

For its Autumn–Winter 2026–2027 Haute Couture collection, Maison Veyrande returns to the hours the city never sees.
They are the white hours: the lamps of the atelier at the top of the house stay lit, and seventeen pairs of hands
work in silence. *Les Heures Blanches* is a collection of thirty-one looks made in that light.

The palette is the atelier's own. Ivory duchesse satin and silk gazar, the colour of the toile before the first cut.
Ink, for the sketch and for the night outside the window. Champagne, for the lamplight on the silk. Nothing else is
needed.

The silhouette is long and still. Coats are cut close to the body and fall straight to the floor. Columns of black
silk faille carry a single line of champagne thread. Evening capes are lined in pleated organza that opens only when
the wearer walks.

The collection closes with *La Dernière Heure*, look 31: an ivory silk organza gown embroidered by hand with
mother-of-pearl sequins and champagne glass beads. It took 1,240 hours to embroider, by seventeen hands, over eleven
weeks. The embroidery follows the lines of the first sketch, stitch for stitch.

> Couture is made in the white hours, when the city sleeps and the hands keep working. I wanted the collection to
> hold that light: one lamp, one length of ivory silk, and the time it takes.
>
> — Odile Sorrèze, Creative Director

The collection is presented on 6 July 2026 in the grand salon of the Maison in Paris, to an original nocturne for
piano and string quartet, performed live. The walk is slow and even: one step to each beat.

### About Maison Veyrande

Founded in Paris in 1927 by Céleste Veyrande, the Maison has dressed its clients by hand for almost a century. Every
piece is drawn, cut, sewn and finished in its own ateliers. The house keeps the craftsmanship of the salon: a fitting
takes an afternoon, a toile is made for every client, and the hand is never hurried.
