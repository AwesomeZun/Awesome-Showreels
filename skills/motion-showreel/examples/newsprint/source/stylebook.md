# The Courier stylebook: house style and design standards (excerpt)

The Tamsin Valley Courier is the daily newspaper of the Tamsin Valley, published in Harlow since 1891. This excerpt
is what the copy desk, the design desk and the video desk share. Where it is silent, follow The Associated Press
Stylebook.

> The Courier, its towns and its people are fictional. This stylebook was written for a motion-showreel example.

## 1. Voice

We write plainly, in the past tense, and we let facts and quotes carry the feeling. A story is warm because of what
people did and said, not because of the adjectives we added.

- Lead with what happened, where and when. Then who it mattered to.
- Attribute with "said," after the name: "Pruitt said." Ages in figures after the name: Walter Pruitt, 81.
- Second reference: last name only. No courtesy titles.
- Every number has a source in the story or a box: records, a vote, a budget, a count we made ourselves.

**Words we do not use:** iconic, beloved, heartwarming, amazing, incredible, miracle, historic (unless a
historian says so), "breaking" for news that is not breaking. No exclamation points in copy or headlines.

## 2. Headlines

- Sentence case, present tense, numerals for every number: `Clock tower ticks again after 31 silent years`.
- No period at the end. No puns in news headlines; features may have one.
- Decks are a full thought in italic, also without a period.

## 3. Datelines and numbers

- Towns in the Courier's coverage area stand alone in capitals, followed by an em dash: `WICKHAM FERRY —`,
  `ASHGROVE —`, `HARLOW —`. Other places take the AP state abbreviation.
- AP numbers: spell out one through nine, figures for 10 and above, always figures for ages, dimensions, money and
  percentages (`6-foot dials`, `$18,250`, `5 percent`). Ordinals: first through ninth, then `10th`, `12th`.
- Times: `noon`, `7 a.m.`, `9 p.m.` Days of the week for the past seven days; `Oct. 11` with a date,
  `January 1995` with a year alone.
- Stories end with `-30-` on the desk copy. Jumps read `See CLOCK, Page A6`.

## 4. The page

The Courier is a six-column broadsheet printed on grey newsprint in two inks: black and one spot red.

| Element | Standard |
|---|---|
| Grid | 6 columns, 1-pica (12 pt) gutters, 0.5-pt column rules between stories |
| Nameplate | `The Tamsin Valley Courier` in Grenze Gotisch Bold, centred between two ears; never stretched, outlined or recoloured except reversed out of black; clear space equal to its cap height |
| Rules | an Oxford rule under the nameplate: 4 pt over 1 pt, black; a 2-pt Courier Red rule under the folio line |
| Headlines | Newsreader, ExtraBold for A1 banners, Bold below the fold, display optical size; leading 0.95 |
| Decks | Newsreader Italic |
| Body | Newsreader text optical size, 9.5 on 10.5 pt, justified and hyphenated; datelines in capitals |
| Labels | Libre Franklin Bold capitals, tracked +80: section flags, folios, sidebars, ears, credits (our Franklin Gothic heritage) |
| Wire and agate | Courier Prime: the Valley Wire ticker, tides, box scores, the police log |
| Photos | black-and-white halftones, 85-line screen at 45°, round dot. We never colourize a photo |

### Colour

Two inks, one paper. Red is a signal, not a decoration: it appears once on a page, on the folio rule, a section
flag or the mark of a pull quote. Never red body copy, never a red photograph, never a red headline.

| Name | Use | Hex (e-edition and video) |
|---|---|---|
| Newsprint | the page | `#E4E0D6` |
| Newsprint shade | the fold, the stack, sunken bands | `#D6D1C5` |

## 5. The Courier on video

Our short videos are the front page in motion: the same grid, type and two inks, and nothing a press could not
print. No gradients, no glow, no colour photos, no drop shadows. Type is set, not thrown: lines land like slugs of
cast metal, firmly and on the beat, without bounce. Pages change the way the press changes them, by rolling.

Our sound is the newsroom: typewriter keys, the teletype and the press, over a steady walking pulse of about 96
beats per minute. No voice-over on teasers under 20 seconds; the type tells the story.
