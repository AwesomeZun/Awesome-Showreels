# Mistfold

*Tea and balm from one quiet hillside.*

![The Mistfold seal: a terracotta ring around a sprig of two leaves and a bud](images/seal.png)

> Mistfold is a fictional brand, written as source material for the `botanical-organic` example of the
> motion-showreel skill. The garden, the family and the products are invented. Nothing here is for sale.

## The hillside

Our grandmother planted the first rows in 1962, on a north-facing slope where the morning mist folds into the valley
and stays until the sun finds it. That fold of mist gave the garden its name. We still pick there, on the same
terraces, from many of the same bushes.

Tea is a camellia, *Camellia sinensis*. Left alone it grows into a small tree. Kept as a garden it becomes waist-high
hedges that follow the contour of the hill like the lines on a map. Cool nights and slow mornings keep the young
leaves tender. We do not force them. A bush that is asked for too much gives thin, bitter tea.

## Grown slowly

From seed to a bush worth picking takes four years. In that time the roots go deep into the loam, the stem turns
woody, and the leaves learn the weather of the hill. We raise our own seedlings from our own seed, a few hundred
every spring, and plant them in the gaps the old bushes leave.

## Picked by hand at first light

We pick only the bud and the two young leaves below it. It is done by hand, before the dew lifts, while the leaves
are cool and full. A picker walks the rows at an easy pace, basket on the hip, and moves on before the sun is high.

The leaves rest in the shade for a few hours, are rolled by hand and dried slowly over low heat. That is all.
Nothing is added.

## A cup at first light

Our spring tea is called First Light. Steep it gently: water at 80 °C, three minutes, two grams to a small cup. The
liquor turns pale gold, and the kitchen smells of cut grass and chestnut. The same leaves will give a second cup,
and a third.

## A balm at dusk

In autumn the bushes flower, small white blossoms with gold at their hearts, and set round brown seeds. We press
those seeds for their oil and stir it with beeswax and shea into a soft balm for hands, elbows and dry cheeks. We call
it Dusk Balm, because that is when most people reach for it.

The same bushes that fill your cup in the morning look after your skin at night.

## What we will not do

- We will not hurry the leaves.
- We will not add perfume to the balm.
- We will not promise what a plant cannot do.
