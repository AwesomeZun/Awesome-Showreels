# A spatial multiome atlas of human alveolar repair resolves a KRT8⁺ transitional niche

*Article · fictional manuscript written for this example · all data illustrative (paper-src/simulate.py)*

Mira K. Halden¹, Joon-ho Seo¹², Ana P. Ferreira³, Tomas Lindqvist¹ & Ruth O. Adeyemi¹³ ✉
¹ Institute for Lung Regeneration (fictional) · ² Department of Computational Biology · ³ Spatial Omics Core

## Abstract
After injury, the human alveolus must rebuild its gas-exchange surface from surviving type 2 cells (AT2). Using paired
single-nucleus RNA and ATAC profiling of 412,806 nuclei from 14 donors, aligned to subcellular-resolution spatial
transcriptomics of matched sections, we resolve a continuous AT2 → AT1 repair trajectory that passes through a
KRT8⁺ CLDN4⁺ SFN⁺ transitional state. In tissue these transitional cells are not scattered: they are 3.2-fold
enriched within 175 µm of injury foci, where they sit beside CTHRC1⁺ fibroblasts and SPP1⁺ macrophages. Ligand–
receptor inference and chromatin accessibility nominate this niche as the place where differentiation stalls. The
atlas is released as an interactive resource.
